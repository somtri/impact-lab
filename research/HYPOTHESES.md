## Source

Felix Patzelt and Jean-Philippe Bouchaud, "Universal scaling and nonlinearity of aggregate
price impact in financial markets," Phys. Rev. E 97, 012304 (2018), arXiv:1706.04163.
Read via the arXiv abstract page and the ar5iv HTML rendering (2026-08-08; PDF text extraction
failed — binary stream — so the PDF itself was not read directly; ar5iv's HTML rendering of the
same arXiv source stood in). Verbatim abstract confirmed:

> "How and why stock prices move is a centuries-old question still not answered conclusively.
> More recently, attention shifted to higher frequencies, where trades are processed piecewise
> across different timescales. Here we reveal that price impact has a universal non-linear
> shape for trades aggregated on any intra-day scale. Its shape varies little across
> instruments, but drastically different master curves are obtained for order-volume and -sign
> impact. The scaling is largely determined by the relevant Hurst exponents. We further show
> that extreme order flow imbalance is not associated with large returns. To the contrary, it
> is observed when the price is "pinned" to a particular level. Prices move only when there is
> sufficient balance in the local order flow. In fact, the probability that a trade changes the
> mid-price falls to zero with increasing (absolute) order-sign bias along an arc-shaped curve
> for all intra-day scales. Our findings challenge the widespread assumption of linear aggregate
> impact. They imply that market dynamics on all intra-day timescales are shaped by correlations
> and bilateral adaptation in the flows of liquidity provision and taking."

Their response function: R_N(Q) := average of (log m_{t+N} - log m_t) conditioned on the signed
aggregate volume Q = sum of q_i = eps_i * v_i over N successive trades, m_t the mid-price
before trade t. They find a rescaled master curve R_N(Q) ~= R_N * F(Q / Q_N) with a sigmoidal
form F(x) = x / (1 + |x|^alpha)^(beta/alpha). Small |x|: near-linear growth (possibly
non-analytic at zero). Large |x|: shape depends on beta — beta=1 gives saturation, beta<1
gives continued (slower) growth, beta>1 gives reversal (impact falls back down at extreme
imbalance — the "arc"). Their cross-instrument fits: alpha = 1.2 +/- 0.6, beta = 1.3 +/- 0.7 —
i.e. beta > 1 is the typical (not universal) finding, so reversal at extreme imbalance is the
modal expectation in their data, not a monotonic saturating (pure square-root) curve.

Their dataset: 12 NASDAQ stocks (2011-2016), 13 OMX Nordic stocks (Oct 2011-Sept 2015), 6 EUREX
futures (Oct 2014-Dec 2015) — equities and listed derivatives, millisecond tick data,
first/last 30 minutes of each session excluded. No volatility-regime or time-of-day analysis is
in the paper, and no randomization/placebo control is reported there. This project applies
their aggregation-and-binning methodology out-of-sample to a different asset class entirely
(Binance USD-M crypto perpetual futures, continuous 24/7 trading, no session boundaries) — that
domain transfer is itself an assumption under test, not inherited support.

## Estimator this project actually runs (pilot-grade, minimal)

Per Brief 014 scope: signed order-flow imbalance (OFI) summed in fixed same-length time bins
(pilot: 1-minute), same-interval log-price response, no rescaling/master-curve fit, no
bootstrap CIs, no regime split, no placebo test. Those are later Stage 3 steps (3-6), not this
one. The predictions below are written at the resolution this pilot estimator can actually
check (shape sign, ordering of extreme bins, one difference number) — not at the resolution
(fitted alpha/beta, CI bands) that later steps will check.

## Hypotheses (falsifiable)

**H1 — Concavity at small-to-moderate imbalance.** Binning trades into 1-minute windows and
plotting mean log-price response against mean OFI per OFI-quantile-bin, the curve is
increasing and concave (not convex, not linear) across the bins spanning roughly the 10th-90th
percentile of |OFI|: each additional unit of imbalance moves price by less than the previous
unit did. Falsified if the fitted local slope (bin-to-bin response delta / bin-to-bin OFI
delta) is flat or increasing across that range.

**H2 — Extreme-imbalance turnover (arc), not monotone saturation.** Given the paper's typical
beta > 1 fits, the outermost 1-2 OFI bins (top and bottom, most extreme imbalance) show a mean
response that is NOT the largest in magnitude — i.e. the most extreme bin's |response| is less
than or equal to the second-most-extreme bin's, on at least one side (buy or sell). This is the
paper's headline, counter-folklore claim (extreme imbalance does not mean extreme price move;
the price is "pinned"). Falsified if both extreme bins strictly extend the trend of the interior
bins (pure monotone concave/saturating curve, no turnover) on both sides.

**H3 — Regime direction: volatility.** Splitting the pilot month into terciles by realized
1-minute return volatility (not built in this pilot lane — Stage 3 step 5), the response curve
in the high-volatility tercile has larger amplitude (steeper near the origin) than the
low-volatility tercile, holding OFI-bin definition fixed, because the paper ties the scaling
exponents to the Hurst exponent of the flow/price process and higher realized volatility
typically co-occurs with a less-persistent (lower-Hurst) local regime. Falsified if amplitude is
flat or inverted across terciles.

**H4 — Regime direction: time-of-day / session activity.** BTC and ETH perpetuals trade 24/7
with no formal session boundary, but volume is not flat across UTC hours. Predicted: response
amplitude (price move per unit OFI) is larger during low-trade-count UTC hours than
high-trade-count UTC hours — thinner order flow moves price more per unit of imbalance,
consistent with a liquidity/impact-scale (not shape) effect. Falsified if amplitude is flat or
inverted between the top and bottom trade-count-hour terciles.

**H5 — Regime direction: funding windows.** Binance USD-M perpetuals settle funding every 8
hours (00:00, 08:00, 16:00 UTC). Predicted: OFI and/or response amplitude in the 5-minute window
immediately before each funding timestamp differs measurably from the same-length window
starting 30 minutes earlier on the same day (funding-driven positioning flow is not random with
respect to clock time). Falsified if the two windows are indistinguishable on both OFI
distribution and response amplitude.

**H6 — Placebo.** Pseudo-orders assigned at random trade-count-matched times (not real trade
timestamps, no real signed volume) must show a response indistinguishable from zero — mean
response near zero at all "pseudo-OFI" bin levels, with no concave or arc structure. This is not
built in this pilot lane (Stage 3 step 6). If a future placebo run instead reproduces H1/H2's
shape, the estimator is confounded (e.g. by a shared clock artifact) and the real-data results
in this stage are void until the confound is found — that outcome ships in the memo as-is, per
PLAN.md Stage 3 step 6.

## Proxy-validation prediction (D-008)

Trade-price returns proxy true L2 midprice returns because BTCUSDT futures spread is
~1 tick / ~10^-6 relative to price (docs/data-spike.md), orders of magnitude below per-1-minute-
bin volatility. Predicted: the trade-price-proxy response curve and the true-midprice response
curve for 2026-07-01 BTCUSDT agree to within the same order of magnitude at every OFI bin, and
the largest per-bin difference is small relative to the bin-to-bin variation in the curve itself
(no formal CI-width comparison at this pilot-grade stage — D-008's revisit condition, proxy
error comparable to a future CI band width, is checked once real CIs exist in Stage 3 step 4).
Falsified if the two curves disagree in sign or in relative magnitude at any bin.
